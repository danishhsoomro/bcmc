\# BC Muslim Counsellors — Homepage Specification



\## 1. Purpose



BC Muslim Counsellors (BCMC) is a discovery and referral resource for qualified counselling professionals in British Columbia who identify as Muslim.



The homepage has one primary job:



Help someone feel comfortable enough to begin looking for a counsellor.



The visitor may:

\- have already decided to seek counselling

\- only be considering counselling

\- know something feels wrong without knowing how to describe it

\- know what they are experiencing but not know what kind of counselling they need

\- simply want to understand what is available



Do not assume the visitor knows clinical terminology, therapy modalities, or what kind of professional they need.



BCMC is NOT inherently:

\- Islamic counselling

\- faith-based therapy

\- spiritually integrated psychotherapy

\- counselling for religious issues



Individual counsellors may offer these services, but Muslim identity and faith-integrated practice must remain separate concepts throughout the product.

\# 2. Experience Principle



The visitor has arrived at our door.



Do not immediately make them navigate a directory alone.



The homepage follows this emotional sequence:



ARRIVAL

↓

PERMISSION

↓

ORIENTATION

↓

TRUST

↓

HUMANITY

↓

AGENCY



There is an important behavioural rule:



If the visitor clicks the hero CTA, they are ready to begin looking.



If they scroll instead, assume:



"I'm not ready to do that yet. Tell me more."



Everything below the hero should respond to that behaviour.



Do NOT repeatedly push the visitor back toward the CTA.

\# 3. Visual Direction



The website should feel:



\- calm

\- warm

\- intelligent

\- contemporary

\- credible

\- human

\- spacious

\- understated



Apple is an inspiration for:

\- hierarchy

\- restraint

\- whitespace

\- typography

\- motion

\- progressive disclosure

\- confidence without visual clutter



Do NOT imitate Apple literally.



Avoid generic:

\- SaaS layouts

\- wellness websites

\- therapist-directory grids

\- Islamic organization websites

\- healthcare portals



Avoid:

\- excessive cards

\- excessive pills/chips

\- gradients used decoratively

\- glassmorphism

\- excessive shadows

\- giant icon grids

\- stock therapy-session photography

\- sad people

\- hands holding

\- tissues

\- meditation imagery

\- obvious religious clichés

\- decorative crescents/mosques

\- excessive Islamic geometric motifs



The BCMC logo may contain Islamic-inspired geometry, but the entire website should not become visually religious.

\# 5. Typography



Use typography as a major part of the visual identity.



Desired combination:



Editorial serif for large emotional statements.

Clean contemporary sans-serif for body/interface text.



Do not use decorative or overtly traditional Islamic typography.



Headlines:

\- large

\- confident

\- generous line-height

\- substantial whitespace



Body:

\- highly readable

\- comfortable line lengths

\- never unnecessarily small



Avoid excessive font weights.



Typography should feel premium through proportion and spacing rather than ornament.

\# 7. SECTION ONE — ARRIVAL



\## Hero



This is the most important section.



Use:



/public/images/hero/bcmc-door-hero.png



The image composition is intentional.



The door should be on the RIGHT.



There should be substantial negative space on the LEFT for copy.



The door represents arrival.



The visual metaphor is:



"The door is open. You can come in."



Do not crop the door out on desktop.



Do not turn the hero into a generic image + text split card.



The photograph should feel integrated into the page.



\## Copy



Headline:



You don't need to know exactly what you need.



Supporting copy:



Tell us what's bringing you here. We'll make it easier to see your options.



\## Primary CTA



Help me find someone



Do not use:

\- Start exploring

\- Begin your journey

\- Take the first step

\- Find your match



\## Secondary possibility



A quiet text link may eventually allow:



Browse all counsellors



Do not visually compete with the primary CTA.



\## Important



The homepage currently communicates guidance.



Do not imply:

\- diagnosis

\- clinical assessment

\- algorithmic matching

\- that BCMC determines the "right" counsellor



The eventual discovery experience helps people search and narrow their options.



\---



\# 8. Hero Motion



Motion should be subtle.



On initial load:



1\. Hero appears naturally.

2\. The door may move/open by a VERY small amount if the implementation can achieve this convincingly.

3\. Headline enters.

4\. Supporting copy follows.

5\. CTA appears last.



Do not create theatrical door animation.



If the image cannot support believable door movement without visual degradation, DO NOT fake it.



A subtle image reveal or crop transition is preferable to bad animation.



Motion should communicate:



"You've arrived."

\# 9. SCROLL ONE — PERMISSION



Remember:



Someone reaching this section saw the CTA and chose not to click.



Do not immediately give them another CTA.



Do not give them filters.



Do not ask them to categorize what they are experiencing.



The job of this section is:



"You can stay here and understand more first."



\## Main statement



Wherever you are right now, you're welcome here.



\## Progressive supporting thoughts



Reveal these individually rather than presenting them as cards:



You might know exactly what you're going through.



You might only know that something doesn't feel right.



You might be wondering whether counselling could help.



You might simply want to see what's available.



The visitor should understand:



I don't need to declare that I need therapy in order to use this website.



\## Design



Large whitespace.



Typography-led.



Minimal decoration.



This section should feel slower than the hero.



Each thought can reveal as the visitor scrolls.



Do not turn these statements into four feature cards.



\---



\# 10. SCROLL TWO — ORIENTATION



The visitor's likely question is now:



"What actually happens here?"



Answer it.



\## Heading



A clearer way to look.



Alternative wording may be refined later, but do not improvise marketing copy.



\## Supporting idea



BCMC gives people useful ways to understand and compare counsellors without requiring them to know professional terminology first.



Explain the journey in THREE steps.



\### 01 — Start with what you know



You don't need to know a therapy type or clinical term.



\### 02 — Narrow what matters



Consider practical and personal preferences such as:

\- location

\- online or in-person

\- language

\- fees

\- availability

\- counsellor gender

\- who they work with



These examples should not become an overwhelming filter wall.



\### 03 — Decide for yourself



See clear counsellor profiles and decide who, if anyone, you would like to contact.



\## Important product principle



BCMC helps people SEE and NARROW their options.



BCMC does not tell people:

"This is the counsellor for you."



The visitor retains agency.



\## Design



Do not use three generic floating SaaS cards.



Prefer:

\- numbered typography

\- connected progression

\- scroll-led transition

\- one step becoming prominent at a time



The visual experience should suggest clarity emerging gradually.



\---



\# 11. SCROLL THREE — TRUST



The likely visitor question:



"Why should I trust what I'm seeing?"



This section can transition into Forest 900 (#123C32).



Use Cream/White typography.



The shift should feel intentional:

emotional reassurance → factual confidence.



\## Heading



Know who you're considering.



\## Trust principle 1



Verified professionals



Supporting explanation:



Counsellors listed on BCMC are qualified Registered Clinical Counsellors (RCCs).



Verification terminology and exact BCACC wording may be refined before production launch.



Do not use "licensed therapist" unless explicitly approved later.



\## Trust principle 2



Clear information



Profiles should make important information visible before contact, including:

\- areas of practice

\- populations served

\- approach

\- languages

\- fees

\- location

\- online/in-person

\- availability

\- relevant preferences



\## Trust principle 3



Private \& secure



This language may remain in the design.



The implementation must eventually ensure that privacy claims match the actual technical architecture.



Public browsing should not require an account.



\## Trust principle 4



Muslim doesn't mean faith-based counselling



Supporting explanation:



Every counsellor listed identifies as Muslim. That does not mean every counsellor provides Islamic or faith-based counselling.



Individual profiles will explain whether and how religion or spirituality is part of the counsellor's practice.



This distinction is important.



Do not represent Muslim identity as a clinical specialization.

\# 12. SCROLL FOUR — HUMANITY



Only now introduce individual counsellors.



The visitor understands:

\- why they're here

\- that uncertainty is acceptable

\- what BCMC does

\- what BCMC does not do

\- why the information can be useful



Now show people.



\## Heading



Meet some of the people you might find here.



\## Counsellor presentation



Use 3 example counsellors.

Each preview should contain only:

Who they commonly work with

A short plain-language description of areas they work with

Location / online status



CTA:



View profile



Do NOT create dense directory cards.



Avoid:

\- 10+ pills

\- modality acronyms everywhere

\- star ratings

\- testimonials

\- popularity rankings

\- "best match" percentages



Use:

\- large natural portraits

\- generous spacing

\- strong typography

\- minimal metadata



Counsellor photography should feel consistent but human.



Do not visually stereotype Muslim identity.

\# 13. SCROLL FIVE — AGENCY



The visitor's final question:



"What happens if I keep going?"



The answer should restore complete control to them.



\## Heading



Whenever you're ready.



\## Supporting copy



Looking doesn't commit you to anything.



Browse. Read. Compare. Come back later if you want.



If someone feels worth speaking to, BCMC will show you how to contact them through their preferred contact method.



Possible methods may eventually include:

\- text

\- practice website



BCMC should not initially sit between the person and counsellor's private conversation.

\# 15. Motion System



Motion should communicate meaning.



Not decoration.



Use restrained scroll-triggered animation.



Potential behaviours:



HERO

\- soft reveal

\- extremely subtle image movement

\- staged copy entrance



PERMISSION

\- statements appear individually as user scrolls



ORIENTATION

\- steps progressively become active

\- connector progresses with scroll



TRUST

\- background transitions into Forest

\- trust principles appear progressively



HUMANITY

\- portraits enter naturally

\- subtle horizontal movement may be used on desktop



AGENCY

\- visual rhythm slows again

\- minimal final reveal



Avoid:

\- excessive parallax

\- bouncing

\- spinning

\- glass effects

\- floating decorative blobs

\- constant movement

\- animation on every small element



Respect prefers-reduced-motion.



\---



\# 16. Responsive Behaviour



Design desktop first but implementation must be responsive.



Desktop:

\- preserve hero image composition

\- copy occupies negative space on left

\- door remains visible on right

\- generous maximum content width



Tablet:

\- preserve hierarchy

\- reduce whitespace carefully

\- do not simply shrink desktop



Mobile:

\- prioritize copy before imagery where necessary

\- hero may stack

\- preserve the concept of the doorway even if crop changes

\- maintain generous touch targets

\- no horizontal overflow

\- avoid tiny text

\- counsellor previews become vertical

\- motion should become simpler



Do not sacrifice usability to preserve desktop composition.



\---



\# 17. Accessibility



Use semantic HTML.



Requirements:

\- keyboard-accessible navigation

\- visible focus states

\- sufficient colour contrast

\- alt text for meaningful images

\- decorative imagery marked appropriately

\- no interaction communicated by colour alone

\- comfortable touch targets

\- reduced-motion support

\- logical heading hierarchy
